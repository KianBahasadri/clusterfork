# Promotional and Brand-Led Interfaces

Use this guide when the dominant task is to establish relevance, trust, desire, or identity and lead to a next step: landing pages, launches, portfolios, campaigns, product stories, and event pages.

“Flashy” is an expression parameter, not the route. Apply the editorial guide to sustained articles, the transactional guide to signup or purchase, and the operational guide to an interactive product console even when they appear inside a campaign.

## Outcome

Deliver a distinctive, memorable proposition that visitors can understand, interrogate, and act on without spectacle hiding evidence, price, conditions, destination, or control.

## First Frame and Narrative

- `[INV]` The first meaningful viewport provides a legible intended entry—a proposition, identity, or deliberate intrigue—with an operable path forward. Intentional mystery is a `[HYP]`, not cover for accidental ambiguity.
- `[CONV]` For a proposition-led page, start by communicating what is offered, for whom, and what meaningful next step is available.
- `[SIG]` Compose one dominant visual idea and, when an action exists, one dominant next step per narrative phase. Keep supporting navigation and secondary actions available at lower salience.
- `[SIG]` Treat “flashy” as concentrated authorship and staged intensity—not continuous maximum contrast. Alternate spectacle with quiet explanation, proof, and action surfaces.
- `[EVID PROMO-2]` Treat the initial composition as consequential because visual-appeal judgments form rapidly. This does not establish a 50-millisecond decision deadline or prove effects on comprehension, trust, or conversion.
- `[EVID PROMO-1]` Preserve enough category structure that novelty remains interpretable. Evidence connecting lower screenshot complexity and higher prototypicality to aesthetic ratings does not mandate minimalism or sameness.
- `[CONV]` Cover the relevant proposition, mechanism, experience, proof, material qualification, and action in an order fitted to audience and traffic intent. Repeat an action when a new argument changes its meaning or confidence, not at arbitrary scroll intervals.
- `[HYP]` Treat mystery copy, nonstandard navigation, WebGL heroes, scroll-telling, cursor effects, kinetic type, and nonlinear sequences as hypotheses with a stated audience prediction and a stable fallback.

## Brand Expression

- `[SIG]` Spend the largest expressive moves on identity and narrative: type, art direction, composition, image, sound, and motion can be bold when their role is legible.
- `[SIG]` Establish a recognizable motif, not a pile of unrelated effects. Reuse a small vocabulary of shapes, transitions, textures, and spatial relationships so the experience feels authored rather than generated.
- `[SIG]` Contrast expressive passages with stable reading and decision zones. The proposition, qualifications, price, and primary action remain legible at rest.
- `[INV]` Distinguish decoration from interaction. A shape that looks pressable, draggable, selectable, or status-bearing must either fulfill that contract or lose the affordance.
- `[EVID ICON-1 ICON-2]` Label unfamiliar navigation and consequential actions. A custom symbol can carry identity before it carries learned meaning.
- `[HYP]` Maximalism, asymmetry, nostalgia, brutalism, dimensionality, or cinematic polish are campaign hypotheses. Test recognition and message accuracy, not whether viewers merely noticed them.

## Claims and Proof

- `[INV]` Every quantitative, comparative, superlative, scarcity, availability, and outcome claim maps to reviewable substantiation with a known owner, scope, and freshness, plus visible qualifications.
- `[REQ AD-1]` When U.S. FTC jurisdiction applies, substantiate express and implied objective advertising claims before dissemination and disclose applicable endorsement relationships clearly and conspicuously. Verify current rules and product-specific obligations; other jurisdictions differ.
- `[INV]` Qualifications cannot contradict the headline or depend on tiny type, low contrast, hover, motion, or a distant footnote.
- `[CONV]` Identify proof by kind, source, scope, and date. A customer-logo wall is identity, not evidence, unless the relationship and relevance of each mark are truthful.
- `[SIG]` Make proof descendable when the audience benefits: a metric can reveal its method, a testimonial its attributable case, and a capability its demonstration or documentation.
- `[INV]` Demonstrations distinguish live product behavior, recorded output, prototype, simulation, and conceptual illustration. Decorative dashboards cannot imply nonexistent capability.
- `[HYP]` Testimonials, customer logos, counters, awards, and proof ordering are audience-specific. Measure accurate recall and calibrated confidence rather than clicks alone.

## Spectacle and Motion

- `[EVID PROMO-3]` Keep unrelated banner-like motion and visual competition away from the proposition and action. Banner experiments support caution about distraction in search tasks, not a ban on expressive animation.
- `[SIG]` Use motion to stage reveal, causality, scale, continuity, transition, or brand character. Once its information has landed, return to a settled state.
- `[REQ A11Y-1]` Meet applicable pause, stop, hide, audio-control, flash, contrast, text-alternative, keyboard, focus, target, reflow, and resize criteria.
- `[CONV]` Honor the platform's reduced-motion preference. Recompose the experience while preserving narrative order, message, and action rather than simply deleting animated content.
- `[INV]` Preserve native scrolling, selection, history, deep links, focus, and the back action. A user cannot become trapped in a scroll animation or custom pointer mode.
- `[HYP]` Treat sound, haptics, camera movement, 3D depth, and continuous ambient effects as hypotheses: state their purpose, provide a way to prevent or stop persistent sensory output, and test whether their value exceeds distraction, sickness, power, and performance costs.

## Action and Ethical Conversion

- `[INV]` An action does exactly what its label promises. Before activation, expose material cost, commitment, destination, account requirement, data use, and recurrence.
- `[INV]` Never use fake urgency or social proof, hidden recurring terms, preselected extras, disguised advertising, obstruction of refusal or cancellation, or visual treatment intended to defeat free choice.
- `[EVID ETHICS-1]` Use observed deceptive-pattern families as an audit vocabulary, not as a substitute for product ethics or current jurisdiction-specific law.
- `[REQ A11Y-1]` The complete form or transaction path satisfies applicable input, error, authentication, and consequential-action requirements—not merely the hero page.
- `[CONV]` Label actions by outcome when the consequence is not already obvious. Prefer “Start free trial” to “Continue” when activation creates a trial.
- `[SIG]` Give the primary action a protected visual treatment and enough local quiet to be recognized as part of the proposition rather than mistaken for an advertisement.
- `[HYP]` Action copy, offer order, proof order, pricing presentation, and repetition are testable. Use qualified completion as a primary measure and errors, reversals, complaints, refunds, and accessibility failures as guardrails.

## Performance and Responsive Art Direction

- `[INV]` Hero media cannot unnecessarily delay or displace the proposition and action. Reserve dimensions, prioritize the actual first-frame asset, and defer below-fold spectacle.
- `[CONV PERF-1]` Set a field-performance budget appropriate to the audience and traffic context, and measure current Core Web Vitals or equivalent platform signals with real-user data. Thresholds are operational guidance, not conversion laws.
- `[HYP]` Every heavy effect should earn its latency, data, energy, and implementation cost through a predefined campaign outcome and harm guardrail.
- `[SIG]` Art-direct each meaningful viewport instead of merely shrinking the desktop composition. Preserve the proposition, proof, qualifications, and action while changing crop, sequence, scale, or interaction.
- `[INV]` Core content and the action path remain usable when optional media, custom fonts, animation, analytics, or enhancement fails.

## Anti-Patterns

- Splash or loading theatre before the proposition becomes available.
- Every region glowing, moving, scaling, or demanding equal attention.
- A primary action styled like a peripheral advertisement or detached from the proposition it advances.
- Vague labels that conceal signup, payment, recurrence, download, or navigation.
- Fake counters, unattributable testimonials, decorative product UI, and unqualified comparisons.
- Footnote-sized qualifications that negate a large headline.
- Scroll hijacking, custom cursors that replace system feedback, or motion-only navigation.
- A reduced-motion mode that deletes content or breaks the narrative.
- Hero video or font loading that moves an action under the pointer.
- Experiments optimized only for clicks while increasing errors, cancellation difficulty, low-quality conversions, or harm.

## Validation

- After brief exposure, can representative visitors accurately state the offer, intended audience, and next step?
- Can they locate price, recurrence, qualifications, compatibility, evidence, and privacy consequences without coaching?
- Does every claim have owned evidence with a clear source, scope, and date?
- Does every action label match its actual destination and commitment?
- Can visitors refuse, back out, cancel, and recover as readily as the product permits?
- Does the complete path work with keyboard, screen reader, zoom, narrow viewport, reduced motion, and optional media disabled?
- Does the first frame remain composed before custom fonts and heavy media finish loading?
- Do real-user performance measures meet the project's stated budget across representative devices and networks?
- For every `[HYP]`, what is the causal prediction, primary outcome, and harm guardrail?
- If spectacle is removed, is the proposition still distinctive and comprehensible?
